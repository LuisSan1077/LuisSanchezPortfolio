Research · FSU eHealth Lab

NLP for Social Determinants of Health

Built a guideline for labeling social and economic factors (like housing, income, and support) in pediatric transplant social worker notes, then trained language models to find them automatically. Our abstract was accepted to the 2025 AMIA Symposium.

My role
Undergraduate researcher and co-author. I annotated notes, tuned BERT and RoBERTa models, and helped with research design and clinical relevance checks.
Main challenges
The guideline has 17 categories, so the team had to agree on fine distinctions, and the models struggled with that level of detail. We also had to coordinate social work experts and an outside annotator from the University of Florida.
Skills I built
Technical: Python, data annotation, fine-tuning transformer models, and measuring results with precision, recall, and F1.
Soft skills: Cross-disciplinary teamwork and presenting technical results to a clinical audience on a research poster.
How it prepares me
Product managers balance scope against quality. This project taught me that a richer framework gives users more value but raises the bar for the technology, and how to work with domain experts to find the right balance.

Product · Web App

Techno Blockz: A Beat Maker You Play

A browser app in two halves: a techno step sequencer where you build a track, and a falling-block game locked to that track. Pieces fall on the beat, so the tempo sets the difficulty, and every line you clear adds an instrument layer. Every sound is synthesized live in the browser, with no audio files.

My role
Product owner. I set the concept and the core loop, chose the art direction from reference images, called the sound design and the difficulty pacing, played every build, and cut what did not work.
Main challenges
Getting the music and the game to reinforce each other instead of just running side by side. The first loop tied instruments to levels, so a full track took far too long to hear. Tying one layer to each line cleared, with a one-minute timer before the newest layer drops out, turned it into something that builds like a real set. Heavy visual effects also buried the blocks, so I pulled them back until the gameplay read clearly again.
Skills I built
Technical: live audio synthesis in the browser, real-time 3D rendering, and reading performance numbers to decide what to cut, especially on phones.
Soft skills: giving clear direction from references, and judging feel over feature count.
How it prepares me
This is a whole product cycle in miniature: a hypothesis about what would be fun, a build, honest testing, and the discipline to remove things that sounded good on paper but lost to the real thing.

Featured case study

Extracting Social Determinants of Health from Social Worker Notes

Role: Undergraduate Researcher, co-authorTeam: Harjith Pradeep, Luis Sanchez, Zhe HeProgram: FSU UROP · eHealth LabTools: Python, BioBERT, RoBERTa, LLMs

1. Problem

Social determinants of health, such as income, education, and social support, account for up to 40% of preventable deaths, yet they are often missing from electronic health records. Models trained without them make biased predictions.

2. Process & Research

We worked with social work experts and an annotator from the University of Florida to write a two-level annotation guideline: trigger words first, then context.

3. Solution

We trained transformer models to extract trigger words, then passed the matching sentences to large language models for deeper context.

4. Results (level-one tags)

17SDoH categories in the guideline
0.78BioBERT F1, strict match
0.80BioBERT F1, sentence level
0.84RoBERTa precision, sentence level

Artifact: annotation guideline excerpt showing how a trigger word gets a first-level tag and a second-level status.

Artifact: precision, recall, and F1 for BioBERT and RoBERTa under strict, relaxed, and sentence-level matching.

5. Lessons Learned

Performance fell short of our goals, likely because the guideline is highly detailed and has many categories. Next, we’ll improve how the models adapt to the data and refine the annotation strategy. The product lesson: a richer framework gives users more value, but it raises the bar for the model, so scope and quality have to be balanced.
